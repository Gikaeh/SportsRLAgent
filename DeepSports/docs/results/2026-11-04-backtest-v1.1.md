# DeepSports model v1.1 backtest: player availability vs v1

**Question.** Do pre-game player-availability and top-N player features (model v1.1) improve
v1, and do they get any closer to the closing line?

**Answer.** Only a little. v1.1 is slightly better than v1 on win probability and margin
(pooled margin MAE -0.03 points, 95% CI -0.057 to -0.002; Brier -0.0004, CI includes 0) and
no different on totals. It is still clearly worse than the closing line on every target
(Brier 0.2101 vs 0.1990, margin MAE 11.20 vs 10.78, total MAE 15.06 vs 14.29). No betting
threshold has a hit rate whose interval clears 52.4%.

## Method
- **Data:** NBA only, both teams real NBA teams, the same 4069 out-of-sample games and 29 monthly
  walk-forward folds as the v1 report (seasons 2024-2027; 2023 is the Elo burn-in and first
  training season). Features were built once with player columns; v1 simply doesn't read them, and
  its numbers reproduce `2026-11-03-backtest-v1.md` exactly.
- **Features** (`src/deepsports/analysis/players.py`), per team per game, from box scores of
  games that finished before tip (FINISH_LAG = 4h, the same queue as team form):
  - *Rotation:* players ranked by minutes per game over the team's last 10 games this season (a
    missed game counts 0). The predicted game is never used to select or rank.
  - *Availability:* among the top-6 / top-3, how many did not play the previous game; top-6 who
    missed 2 of the last 3; missing-minutes share (absent players' mpg / top-6 mpg); games since
    each top-3 player last played (capped at 5). "Did not play" = absent from the box score or
    `did_not_play`.
  - *Quality:* top-6 per-game plus-minus sum, top-3 points per game, std of top-6 points per
    game, and top-6 plus-minus counting only players who played the previous game. Per-game
    rates are shrunk by 2 prior games (plus-minus toward 0, points toward 10).
  - Win/margin get the home - away differences (9 features); the total model gets
    `miss_share_sum` and `top3_pts_sum`.
- **Model v1.1** = v1's logistic/ridge models with the same fixed settings (C = 1, alpha = 10),
  feature lists extended. Not tuned on the backtest.
- **Scoring:** the existing harness, plus paired per-game differences (v1.1 - v1) with normal 95%
  intervals, out-of-sample permutation importance (shuffle a feature within each test block, 5
  repeats), grouped importance (correlated features shuffled together), and a feature-group
  ablation.

## Leakage checks
1. **Real data, scrambled box scores:** in an in-memory copy, the box scores of 535 games (at most
   one per team-season) were rewritten (DNP flipped, minutes and plus-minus inverted). 0/535 of
   those games' own features changed; 969/987 of the teams' next games did (the rest cross a
   season boundary), so the check has teeth.
2. **Tests** (`tests/test_players.py`): rewriting the predicted game's minutes/DNPs leaves its
   features identical; a rematch 1h later sees no player history; the fold check passes.
3. **Sanity:** the new features' correlations with the margin are modest (top6_pm_diff 0.35,
   about the same as team form at 0.35; availability features -0.05 to -0.10), and v1.1 does not
   approach, let alone beat, the closing line.

## What the importance numbers mean
`top6_pm_diff` and `avail_top6_pm_diff` correlate 0.99 with each other and 0.96 with
`form_net_diff`: summing the plus-minus of the top-6 roughly rebuilds the team's net margin. The
model puts offsetting weights on the pair, so shuffling one alone looks very costly (log loss
+0.06) even though adding them barely helps. The grouped table is the fairer view: each player
group matters about as much as team form inside v1.1, far less than Elo (the quality group is
partly standing in for team form). The ablation shows where the *gain* comes from: **v1 +
availability alone gets all of v1.1's improvement** (Brier 0.2102, margin MAE 11.199), while v1 +
player quality is no better than v1. This
differs from the friend's analysis, likely because their models didn't have a team-form feature.

## Caveats
- Availability is a proxy: "missed the previous game" catches multi-game injuries but not
  same-day scratches, returns from injury, or rest days announced at noon, which is exactly what the
  closing line knows.
- Trades: a player's minutes count only for the team he played them for, so a newly traded star
  starts with a short window at his new team.
- 2027 has only 107 test games; its larger improvement is noise-sized.
- Eight 2026 playoff games have no box scores; those team-games are skipped rather than read
  as "everyone missed".
- Injury hook (`injury_override=True`, off by default, off in the backtest): for scheduled games
  with a pre-tip `pregame_injuries` snapshot, a top-6 player counts as missing iff he is listed Out.
  It changes 6 of 103 current predictions slightly. There's no history to validate it on yet, and
  the model was trained on the proxy, so its effect is untested.

## Next steps
- Use v1.1 for `deepsports predict` only as a modest upgrade (or run it side by side as its own
  `model_version` and compare graded records); it is not an edge.
- Drop the two plus-minus sums from a later version (redundant with team form), or regularize them as a
  group, and re-run.
- Once a few months of pre-tip injury snapshots exist, backtest the injury override and a learned
  injury-impact score on those games only.
