# DeepSports Ensemble v2 (Elo + v1.0 + v1.1 + boosted trees): locked-holdout result

**Question.** Does blending Elo, model v1.0, model v1.1 and a boosted-trees model beat v1.1, and does it
get closer to the closing line, on a season that no tuning or weighting ever saw?

**Answer: no, don't ship.** On the 2025-26 holdout (1,322 games, scored once) the ensemble is
indistinguishable from v1.1: log loss 0.6009 vs 0.6003 (diff +0.0006, 95% CI −0.0017 to +0.0029), margin
MAE 11.37 vs 11.38 (−0.007, CI −0.046 to +0.032). It fails the pre-declared ship rule. It's still far
behind the closing line (log loss +0.024, CI +0.014 to +0.035; margin MAE +0.36, CI +0.19 to +0.55).
Boosted trees on their own are worse than the linear models (log loss 0.6048, margin MAE 11.53), and the
validation-fitted blend gave them almost no weight on win probability.

## Holdout protocol (declared in docs/DECISIONS.md before any fitting)
- **Holdout:** every completed NBA game of season 2026 (2025-26): regular season, NBA Cup, play-in and
  playoffs. 1,322 games have feature rows (the same 1,322 as the v1/v1.1 reports); 1,316 have a closing
  moneyline and spread.
- **Validation (all tuning):** walk-forward monthly folds on 2023-24 and 2024-25 (seasons 2024, 2025;
  2,640 games). 2021-22 is the Elo burn-in, 2022-23 is training only, 2026-27 isn't used.
  `ensemble.validate` drops every row from season ≥ 2026 before folding (tested).
- **Frozen config:** `models/ensemble_v2/config.json` (sha256 `62447f38…b02688`). The holdout step
  writes a marker and refuses to run twice; its per-game predictions are saved and re-rendered with the
  `report` step instead of refitting.
- **Holdout predictions are still walk-forward:** each 2025-26 month's base models train only on games
  that tipped off before that month (as production would), with the frozen hyperparameters and blend weights.
- **Ship rule (declared up front):** better than v1.1 on log loss *or* margin MAE with a 95% day-clustered
  bootstrap CI excluding 0, and not worse on the other.
- **What touched the holdout:** only the single scoring run. Known contamination: v1.0/v1.1's feature
  lists were fixed before their own backtests, but those reports (which we read) include 2026. The trees,
  the blend methods and the blend weights were never fit or scored on 2026.

## Method
- **Base models** (all on `build_features(include_players=True)` rows: pre-tip only, no odds):
  - Elo: `elo_p_home` and `elo_margin` from the existing Elo (k=20, 100-point home edge; untuned).
  - v1.0 and v1.1: the existing logistic/ridge models (`make_model`), unchanged.
  - Trees: scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`/`Regressor` (already in the env; no new
    dependency) on all 27 team + player features, NaNs native, `elo_diff` monotone for win/margin, no early
    stopping, fixed seed. 18 settings tried on validation in two rounds (round 1's best sat at the most
    regularized corner, so round 2 went further; validation only). Picked per target: win
    lr 0.02 / 150 iters / 4 leaves / min leaf 120 (val log loss 0.6227 vs v1.1's ~0.608); margin
    lr 0.03 / 200 / 7 / 120 (val MAE 11.203); total lr 0.01 / 300 / 7 / 250 (val MAE 14.940).
- **Blends** per target: `mean` (plain average), `convex` (non-negative weights summing to 1, on logits for
  win; fit by log loss / MAE), `stack` (logistic regression on logits / OLS on points, with intercept), each
  on two input sets (all bases, or v1.1 + trees). Each candidate was fit on 2024 out-of-sample predictions
  and scored on 2025 (forward in time); the winner was refit on 2024 + 2025 and frozen:
  - win: stack, logit = 1.023·logit(v1.1) + 0.020·logit(trees) − 0.108
  - margin: stack, 0.943·v1.1 + 0.152·trees − 0.560
  - total: mean of v1.1 and trees
  Validation gains over v1.1 alone were already tiny (2025 log loss 0.6087 → 0.6082; margin MAE 11.071 →
  11.060). The holdout says even that didn't carry over.
- **Scoring:** Brier, log loss, accuracy (pick = p ≥ 0.5), margin MAE, 10-bin reliability and ECE. CIs are
  paired percentile bootstraps resampling whole game days (212 days, 2,000 resamples), since games on one
  day share news shocks.

Elo is overconfident (its high bins win less often than predicted). The trees are underconfident (almost
nothing below 0.2 or above 0.9). The ensemble's −0.108 intercept, learned on 2023-25, shifts it toward the
away team; in 2025-26 that was the wrong way (in the 0.4–0.7 bins home teams won more than it said). That
cost it ~1 point of pick accuracy versus v1.1 (CI just touches 0) and is most of why it doesn't help.

By month, the ensemble beat v1.1 on log loss in 4 of 9 months and on margin MAE in 5 of 9: no pattern.

## Caveats
- One holdout season, ~1,300 games: the CIs above are about ±0.002 log loss and ±0.04 margin points, so a
  real gain smaller than that can't be seen. Validation suggested a gain of about that size; it didn't
  appear.
- The tree grid was searched in two rounds on validation (both before the holdout); the round-2 settings
  barely changed validation scores, so it doesn't explain anything.
- Blend selection used one forward split (fit 2024, score 2025): ~1,300 games choosing among 18 candidates,
  with tiny gaps between them. The stack's intercept is the kind of parameter that drifts season to season.
- Not tried: a "market-aware" variant (closing line as an input) and per-game injury news. The gap to the
  close is information the market has and we don't, not modeling (the same finding as v1.1).

## Recommendation
**Don't ship.** Keep v1.1 in `deepsports predict`, the API and the apps. The ensemble adds a model to
maintain and gives no measurable improvement. Boosted trees don't help on ~4–5k training games with these
features. Better next steps are new information (pre-tip injury snapshots once a few months accumulate;
lineup and rest news), or the college market, where a public-data model might be closer to the line.
